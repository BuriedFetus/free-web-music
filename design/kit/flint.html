How it fits in the studio

Flint is one more instrument column in the song arrangement. Its patterns (Groove, Fill…) go in TRACK cells like Basalt's. Click a Flint cell and the Piano roll panel becomes this drum grid. A drum pattern is as long as its longest row; shorter rows repeat inside it and start again with each TRACK. Try clicking the Drums cells on the left.

  • A kit is 8 pads. Any pad can be any drum: four kicks, two snares and two open hats is fine (try the "Four on the floor" kit).
  • Every drum comes in 4 characters: Analog (round, warm), Digital (crisp, a little gritty), Noise (airy, made from noise), Vintage (thick, saturated).
  • The step grid works like the sequencer you liked: one row per pad, each with its own length (here the rim runs 12 steps and the perc 7 against 16).
  • Tools: Draw switches steps on and off; Accent marks a step to play louder (by the Accent knob on Flint); Roll makes a step play 2, 3 or 4 quick hits (click again to change). The lane under the grid sets each step's velocity for the selected row: drag across it.
  • M / S on each row mutes or solos that pad (soloing one or more rows plays only those).
  • Changing a pad's sound marks the kit "edited"; in the real Flint you can save it as your own kit.
  • Samples: later. The goal is a pad that plays your own sample without everyone downloading it from a server.
