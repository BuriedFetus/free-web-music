How it fits in the studio

  • Chords follow the song's Key. Each strip is a chord of the key (in C major: C, Dm, Em, F, G, Am, B°). The chord type (Triad, 7th, sus2, sus4, add9, Power) changes all of them and stays in the scale, so in C major a 7th on E gives Em7 and on G gives G7.
  • Inside a strip, each string plays its note of that chord, as a guitarist would finger it (× = that string is not in the chord: it plays a muted thud if you drag over it). Drag down or up across the strings to strum; the faster you drag, the harder it plays. Click a chord name to strum it.
  • Notes mode is the neck itself, frets 0–12: the scale's notes are lit, the key's root is the brightest. Drag along a string to slide. "Only scale notes" stops wrong notes.
  • The sound is a modelled string, not a recording: a pluck runs round a tuned loop that loses its brightness as it rings. Pick sets where and how hard you pluck; Body is the guitar it's on (nylon, steel, hollow jazz box, or electric pickup), then the amp: Drive, Cab, Presence.
  • In the studio Garnet is an instrument like Basalt: its patterns sit in the song arrangement and the Piano roll edits them. What you strum here is recorded as notes, each string a few milliseconds after the last, so the strum plays back the way you played it. Knobs are shared and show who is turning them; the mode and chord type are yours.
  • Six strings means six notes at once. That polyphony is new in the engine, and Opal (pads) and Quartz (keys) will use it too.
  • With the song Key set to "No key", chords use C major until a key is picked.
